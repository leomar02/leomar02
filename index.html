Duas culturas · Dos culturas

One family brings the tiles and the fado. The other brings the merengue and the bizcocho.

Everything on this page borrows from both. Here's what you're looking at.

PortugalFrom Lisbon's walls and streets

  • AzulejosThe blue-and-white glazed tiles that cover Lisbon's facades. Our tiles up top are drawn the same way, with the corners joining into circles when they meet.
  • Calçada portuguesaHand-laid black and white stone paving. The wave band under the hero copies the "mar largo" pattern of Rossio square.
  • FadoLisbon's song of saudade, played on the pear-shaped guitarra portuguesa. Expect one during cocktails.
  • Pastéis de nataCustard tarts with a scorched top. They'll come out late, next to the cake.

República DominicanaFrom Santo Domingo and the island

  • LarimarA blue stone with white veins, found only in the Dominican Republic. It's the light blue that runs through this page.
  • FlamboyánThe tree that turns the island red-orange from May to July. In June it'll be in full bloom, so it gave us our coral.
  • Merengue & bachataTambora, güira and accordion. The dance floor opens with merengue and doesn't stop.
  • Bizcocho dominicanoThe Dominican wedding cake: soft, pineapple-filled, under a cloud of suspiro meringue.
