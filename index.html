Our story

We met in New York, two people with families an ocean apart who kept finding the same things at home in each other: big Sunday tables, music that never turns off, and a grandmother in each family who insists you eat more.

Seven years later we're bringing both families together. One brings the tiles and the fado. The other brings the merengue and the bizcocho. Everything on this page borrows from both, and here's what you're looking at.

PortugalFrom Lisbon's walls and streets

  • AzulejosThe blue-and-white glazed tiles that cover Lisbon's facades. Our tiles up top are drawn the same way, with the corners joining into circles when they meet.
  • Calçada portuguesaHand-laid black and white stone paving. The wave band under the hero copies the "mar largo" pattern of Rossio square.
  • FadoLisbon's song of saudade, played on the pear-shaped guitarra portuguesa. Expect one during cocktails.
  • Pastéis de nataCustard tarts with a scorched top. They'll come out late, next to the cake.
  • Vinho do PortoPortugal's national drink: sweet fortified wine from the Douro valley, aged in the cellars of Porto. We'll toast with it.

República DominicanaFrom Santo Domingo and the island

  • LarimarA blue stone with white veins, found only in the Dominican Republic. It's the light blue that runs through this page.
  • FlamboyánThe tree that turns the island red-orange from May to July. In June it'll be in full bloom, so it gave us our coral.
  • Merengue & bachataTambora, güira and accordion. The dance floor opens with merengue and doesn't stop.
  • Bizcocho dominicanoThe Dominican wedding cake: soft, pineapple-filled, under a cloud of suspiro meringue.
  • MamajuanaThe Dominican national drink: rum, red wine and honey steeped in a bottle of tree bark and herbs. Shots at the bar all night.

Agenda

An afternoon in two languages. The ceremony is short and the party is long.

  1. 3:30 pm

    Welcome Boas-vindas · Bienvenida

    Vinho verde and morir soñando in the garden.

  2. 4:00 pm

    Ceremony Cerimónia · Ceremonia

    Under the glass atrium, about 30 minutes.

  3. 5:00 pm

    Cocktail hour Petiscos · Picaderas

    Pastéis de bacalhau and tostones, with a fado guitarist on the rooftop.

  4. 6:30 pm

    Dinner Jantar · Cena

    Family style: bacalhau à Brás and pernil, side by side.

  5. 8:00 pm

    Cake & toast Bizcocho · Brinde · Brindis

    Cutting the bizcocho dominicano, then a toast with both national drinks: a glass of Port and a shot of mamajuana.

  6. 8:30 pm

    Dancing Baile

    Merengue and bachata until 11, with at least one Portuguese pimba song for the uncles.
